Design specification

Clinical Clarity

The brand and interface system for the Ostomy Diary app. This document is the reference for designers and developers: it defines the palette, type, spacing, components and content rules, and states which decisions are fixed and which are open.

Applies to
iOS and Android patient app, care-team report, marketing site
Owner
Product design. Raise changes as a PR against this document.
Reference build
Ostomy Mobile Mockups, screens 1a–1v

1. Position and principles

Ostomy patients manage a continuous clinical condition. The interface has to hold two things at once: enough medical authority that a patient trusts the numbers and a physician accepts the report, and enough lightness that logging six times a day does not feel like a chore. Clinical Clarity resolves that as a crisp, card-based, high-legibility system where saturated colour is rationed and reserved for clinical meaning.

Legibility before decoration
Numbers are read in bathrooms, at night, one-handed. Size, contrast and tabular alignment win over styling.
Logging above reporting
Quick-add sits at the top of the dashboard. Analysis is one tap away, never in the way of the entry.
Colour carries meaning
Red means a clinical anomaly. Teal means an action or a confirmation. Neither is ever used decoratively.
The app observes, it does not diagnose
Ranges are shown with their source and described as suggestions. Advice belongs to the care team.

2.1 Rules that are not negotiable

  • Red is a clinical signal. It marks a value outside the patient's range, a flagged day, or a required field that is blocking a save. It is never a brand colour, a chart default, or a delete-button fill by habit.
  • Teal at #14B8A6 is a fill colour. As text or a thin icon on white it drops to #0F766E for contrast.
  • Never use colour as the only signal. Every flagged value also carries a word — "Above range", "Flagged", "Low" — or an icon.
  • One saturated element per card. If a card holds both a teal action and a red flag, the flag wins and the action reverts to the secondary style.
  • No gradients, no tinted photography, no colour-coded backgrounds for whole screens.

2.2 Data-series colours

Charts use the same three roles everywhere, so a physician reading the report and a patient reading Trends see one language: output is slate #475569, fluid intake is teal #14B8A6, urine is a dashed #CBD5E1 line, thresholds are a dashed #FECACA rule, and any bar or point that breaches the threshold turns #B91C1C.

4. Layout, shape and elevation

Spacing

A 4px base: 4, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16, 20, 24. Screen gutter is 20px on both platforms. Cards are 14–16px inside. Vertical rhythm between card groups is 12–14px; between a section label and its content, 8–9px.

Radius

Cards and sheets 14px. Buttons, inputs and segmented controls 12px. Small controls and inner tiles 10px. Pills and chips 999px. Nothing is square, nothing is more rounded than 14px except pills.

Elevation

Two steps only. Resting card: 0 1px 2px rgba(15,23,42,.04) with a 1px #E2E8F0 border. Raised (segmented thumb, sheet, toggle knob): 0 1px 2px rgba(15,23,42,.08). No blurred, coloured or neumorphic shadows.

Structure

One white card per idea. Related rows live inside one card separated by #F1F5F9 hairlines rather than becoming separate cards. Bottom bars and sheet headers are white against the #F8FAFC ground with a single 1px top or bottom border.

5. Components

Buttons

Primary is a solid teal fill, one per screen, 52px tall in a footer bar and full width. Secondary is white with a #CBD5E1 border. Ghost is text-only in muted slate. Field labels are uppercase 11.5/600 muted, above the control. Inputs are 50–52px tall, 16px text on mobile so iOS does not zoom. Two mutually exclusive options use a segmented control with a white raised thumb; three or more that are not exclusive use chips. Selected chips are a solid teal fill; a selected card-sized option is the #F0FDFA tint with a 1.5px #14B8A6 border.

Status pills
Above range Low SYNCED EST.

Four and only four: alert (clinical breach), caution (worth noticing), confirmation (system state that is fine), neutral (a fact about the record, such as Measured or Estimated). A pill never wraps to two lines — shorten the label instead.

Quick-add tiles

A two-column grid of 72–88px cards, 10px gap. The most-used entry is the single teal-filled card; the rest are white with a #E2E8F0 border and a teal category label. The last cell is a dashed-border "Something else". The amount is the largest thing on the card, tabular, with the unit and method trailing in muted 12.5. Three treatments were explored (cards, list rows, stepper rows); the card grid is the shipped one.

Bottom navigation

Five destinations: Today, Add, Trends, History, You. White bar, 1px #E2E8F0 top border, 52px minimum item height plus the platform safe area. Active is #0F766E icon and 600 label; inactive is #94A3B8 with a 500 label. Labels are sentence case at 10.5px and always present.

Alert and information panels

A tinted card with a matching 1px border, an icon at 20px, a 14/600 heading and 12.5/400 body. Alert panels use the red family, caution the amber family, information the teal family, and a plain white card with a #E2E8F0 border for neutral notes. Panels never stack more than one per screen region; if two conditions apply, show the clinical one.

6. Data display

  • Volumes are whole numbers in mL, thousands separated by a comma (1,640 mL). The unit is never bolded and never larger than the figure.
  • Net balance is signed with a true minus (−740), coloured #B91C1C when negative beyond the patient's threshold and ink otherwise.
  • Urine is reported on its own and excluded from net balance. Any screen showing balance must say so.
  • Every volume entry carries Measured or Estimated. It is a required field and shows as a neutral pill in lists.
  • Times are 24-hour, tabular, in faint slate at the left of a row. Dates read "Thu 4 Sep"; formal report headers read "4 Sep 2026".
  • Ranges appear with their source and a qualifier: "Suggested 600–1,200 mL", "Typical for an ileostomy 3 weeks after surgery".
  • Edited entries keep both versions visible with the change, the author and the timestamp.

7. Accessibility

  • Touch targets are 48px minimum, 52px for primary actions and anything used at night.
  • Body text is 14px minimum and honours the platform text-size setting up to 200%; layouts wrap rather than truncate.
  • Text contrast meets WCAG AA against its own background. #94A3B8 is only used for metadata at 11.5px and above, never for instructions.
  • Teal on white is a fill colour, not a text colour: use #0F766E for teal text and small icons.
  • Colour is never the sole carrier of meaning; every status pill and chart flag has a text label.
  • Keyboard and switch focus is a 2px #14B8A6 outline at 2px offset — never the browser default.

8. Voice

Plain, second person, unhurried. Say what happened and what it means for the log, not how the patient should feel. No exclamation marks, no streaks, no congratulation for logging. Clinical terms appear with a plain-language gloss the first time: "Anti-diarrheal — loperamide. Slows output. Sold as Imodium."

Write this
"Your output is high and your fluids are 1,500 mL short of your goal."
"That's a lot for one emptying. Please check the amount is right before you save."
Not this
"Warning! Dehydration risk detected."
"Great job logging 5 days in a row!"
"Invalid input."

10. Open questions

  • Dark mode is not yet specified. Night logging currently relies on the light theme at reduced brightness; a dark palette needs its own contrast pass before it ships.
  • The care-team web view reuses these tokens but needs a denser table scale and a print stylesheet.
  • Iconography is Lucide at 2px stroke pending a licence decision; sizes in use are 14, 18, 20 and 21px.
  • No illustration or photography style has been set. Nothing in the app currently requires either.
